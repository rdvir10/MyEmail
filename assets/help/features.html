What it can do

Everything MyEmail offers, listed for the tablet and for the phone, since the same app gives you more where there is more screen.

Version 2.48.0Read the user manual →

Version 2.48.0. The same app runs on the phone and the tablet; what changes is how much screen there is to put things on. This file lists what each one offers. The user manual (User manual.md) says how to use it.

Three sizes decide the layout, and the app picks by itself:

On the tablet

Everything in "On the phone" below works here too, plus what the extra screen makes possible.

Layout

  • Folders, message list and the message open at once (sideways).
  • The reading pane can sit to the right, below the list, or be turned off.
  • Drag the dividers to resize the panes; the widths are remembered.
  • Hide the folder pane to give the list and message more room.
  • A command ribbon across the top (sideways only): Sync, New email, New meeting, Delete, Reply, Reply all, Forward, Quick Steps, Move, Read/Unread, Pane, Search, Settings. It scrolls if the screen is narrow.
  • The same three-dot view-and-sort menu, beside the folder name.

With a keyboard

The whole app is drivable without touching the screen. F1 lists every key.

  • Getting around: F6 moves between folders, list and message; Ctrl+. and Ctrl+, step through messages; Ctrl+Shift+I jumps to the Inbox.
  • In the list: arrows, Home/End, Page Up/Down, Enter to open, Space to tick, Shift+arrows to tick a run, Ctrl+A for everything on screen, Delete.
  • In the folder tree: arrows walk the folders and open them as you go; → and ← expand and collapse.
  • While reading: arrows, Space, Page keys, Home/End scroll; F11 full screen; Esc back to the list.
  • Commands: Ctrl+N, Ctrl+Shift+M, Ctrl+R, Ctrl+Shift+R, Ctrl+F, Ctrl+Alt+F, Ctrl+D, Ctrl+Q, Ctrl+U, Insert, Ctrl+Shift+V, Ctrl+E, Ctrl+P, F9.
  • Writing: Ctrl+Enter sends, Esc closes.

Windows (and DeX)

  • Open a message in a window of its own, or write in one, so the mailbox stays visible behind it.
  • Two messages side by side.
  • A setting to make every new message open in its own window.

Drag and drop

  • Drag a message onto a folder to move it.
  • Drag folders to re-nest or reorder them.
  • Drag an attachment out to another app in split screen.
  • Drag a message out as an .eml file into another app or another MyEmail window; drop one back onto a folder to move it.
  • Drop a file from another app onto a message you are writing to attach it.

Mouse or trackpad

  • Right-click a message for the full menu: reply, reply all, forward, forward as attachment, copy, create event, select, move, read, flag, delete.
  • Right-click a conversation row for the same, applied to the whole thread.

On the phone

Mail

  • Several accounts at once — Gmail (Google sign-in, or an app password) and Outlook/Microsoft 365 (sign-in).
  • One Inbox that merges every account, or each account's folders on their own.
  • Full folder tree, nested, with unread and total counts on every folder (14/2310).
  • Favourites at the top; folders you never use can be hidden.
  • Works offline for anything already fetched: lists, messages you have opened, and their attachments.
  • The list loads more as you scroll to the bottom, not just the newest fifty.
  • Each row: who it is from with their address, a ↩ or → when you replied or forwarded, unread rows on white and read ones on grey, a one-tap flag, and times in the phone's own 12- or 24-hour clock.
  • The bar above the list names the folder, whose it is and how many are unread; search is a magnifier there rather than a box taking a row of the screen.

Reading

  • Tap a message and it opens on its own screen; back returns to the list.
  • Conversations: a thread folds into one row you can open, or stays off.
  • Full screen reading — the body alone with the Android bars hidden.
  • Pictures are blocked until you ask, and a sender you trust loads them for good. Pictures sent inside the message, such as a pasted screenshot or a signature's logo, always show.
  • A message keeps its own look: the colours, fonts and right-to-left layout it was sent with.
  • Who it went to, folded to one line; one tap shows every name and address in full.
  • Text size: Small, Default, Large or Extra large, for the whole app and the messages in it.
  • Attachments: open, save, share, or copy to the clipboard. Opening one offers the apps that handle that kind of file, and goes straight to your usual one where you have set a default. They fold away to a one-line count, and pictures the message already shows are not listed twice.
  • Move to a folder from the message itself, beside Delete.
  • Print, or save as PDF.
  • Meeting invitations show as a card with Accept, Tentative and Decline, and can be added to your calendar.
  • New meeting: pick the account, the people, when and where; the invitation goes out from that account's own calendar (Outlook, or Google Calendar with Google sign-in) and the replies collect there. Any message can become one, filled in from it.
  • Save a message exactly as it arrived, or copy it as a file.

Writing

  • New message, reply, reply all, forward, and forward as attachment.
  • Rich editor: bold, italic, underline, bullet and numbered lists, and the quoted original stays editable.
  • Recipients suggested as you type, from your contacts and from everyone you have mailed.
  • Choose which account a message goes from, even mid-reply.
  • A signature per account, written in the same editor, pictures included.
  • Attach files, paste a file from the clipboard, and send from the share sheet of any other app.
  • Drafts are saved to the server, so they are also on the web and in Outlook.
  • A half-written message is kept in Drafts if Android closes the app while you are away from it.
  • Replies go to the Reply-To address when the sender gave one, and stay in the conversation for everyone on it.

Sorting, finding, tidying

  • A three-dot menu on the app bar: sort by date (newest or oldest), sender or subject; conversations on or off; row density; and through to all view settings.
  • Search across subject, sender and body, scoped to a folder, an account, or everywhere — on the server, not just what is cached.
  • Search results can be ticked, select-all'd, then deleted, moved or forwarded.
  • Swipe a message — or a whole conversation — left or right; you choose what each direction does.
  • Long-press to start ticking; act on the lot at once.
  • Move, delete, flag, mark read or unread.
  • Undo on the message that says what happened, for anything moved or deleted.
  • Reply, Reply all and Delete on the notification itself, without opening the app.
  • Quick Steps: named chains of actions (mark read, flag, move, delete) applied in one tap.
  • Create, rename, move and delete folders; mark all read; empty Trash and Junk.

Keeping up

  • Background sync: only when you open the app, occasionally, every five minutes, or push as it arrives.
  • Notifications per account, with a master switch and per-account mute. Each one shows the sender's initial on a circle in the account's colour.
  • Home screen widgets: an app-icon-sized badge per mailbox showing what is new and what is there in total, in its account's colour or one you pick, with a name you choose; tapping one opens that folder.
  • Updates: the About screen checks for a new version and installs it.

What the phone does not have

  • No side-by-side panes, no ribbon: the phone shows one thing at a time by design.
  • The reading-pane setting has no effect there — a message always opens as its own screen.
  • Everything else, including sort, search, widgets, notifications, printing, invitations and full screen, works the same on both.
