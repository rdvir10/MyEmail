1. Getting started

Adding an account

Settings → Accounts → Add, or the app asks on first run.

  • Gmail needs an app password, not your ordinary one. docs/gmail-app-password.md walks through making one. Two-step verification has to be on first.
  • Outlook / Microsoft 365 signs you in the normal way, in a browser window, and no password is stored here.

Passwords and tokens go into the Android Keystore. They are never written to a file, and they are not in the settings backup unless you ask for that and set a passphrase.

Removing one

Settings → Accounts → Remove. It says what goes with it — the cached mail for that account — and asks before doing it.

2. What is on screen

Phone

  • Top bar: the folder you are in, the ☰ drawer for folders, and ⋮ for view and sort.
  • Search box under it.
  • The list, one message per row.
  • ✏️ button, bottom right, for a new message.
  • Tapping a message opens it on its own screen. Back returns to the list.

Tablet, upright

Folders on the left, the list beside them. A message opens on its own screen, or below the list if you set the reading pane to Below.

Tablet, sideways

Folders, list and the open message, all at once, under the command ribbon: Sync, New email, Delete, Reply, Reply all, Forward, Quick Steps, Move, Read, Pane, Search, Settings.

  • Drag the lines between the panes to resize them; the sizes are remembered.
  • The ⇤ button beside the folder name hides the folder pane and brings it back.
  • Pane in the ribbon moves the message pane: right, below, off.

3. Reading mail

The list

Bold means unread. The blue dot is the same thing. A paperclip means an attachment, a flag means flagged, and the number in a pill (2) means a conversation of that many messages.

  • Folders show unread/total, so 14/2310 is fourteen unread of two thousand three hundred and ten.
  • Scroll to the bottom and the next fifty load by themselves.
  • Pull down to check for new mail.

Conversations

Off unless you turn them on (⋮ → Conversations). On, a thread is one row; tap it to open the thread, tap again to close. Everything you can do to a message — swipe, menu, tick — a conversation row does to the whole thread, and says how many that is.

The message

Reply, Reply all, Forward, Read/Unread, Delete and ⋮ are along the top. On the phone, Delete takes the flag's place and the flag moves into the ⋮ menu.

The ⋮ menu holds: Open in new window, Print or save as PDF, Create calendar event, Save source, and Flag on the phone.

Full screen

The ⛶ button (or F11) gives the message the whole display, with the Android bars hidden — worth it for a newsletter laid out for a wide page. A bar with the subject shows briefly and comes back on a tap. Esc, F11 or Back leaves.

Pictures

Pictures are not fetched until you say so, because fetching one tells the sender you read the message, when, and roughly where you are.

  • Show images loads them this once.
  • The ⋮ beside it offers Always show from sender or Always show from everyone at domain — the second is the one for shops, which send from a different address every time.
  • Settings → View → Senders you trust lists what you have allowed, with a way to take any of it back.
  • Settings → View → Load pictures automatically turns blocking off entirely.

Attachments

Chips under the header. Tap one to open it. Long-press picks it up so you can drag it into another app in split screen. Its menu has save, share and copy.

Printing

⋮ → Print or save as PDF, or Ctrl+P. Android's print sheet opens, and "Save as PDF" is one of the printers in it. The page gets a header block — subject, from, to, date — then the message as it was.

Meeting invitations

An invitation shows as a card under the header: what, when, where, who asked. Accept, Tentative or Decline answers the organiser — through the Outlook calendar for a Microsoft account, and by the standard calendar reply for Gmail. Add to calendar hands the event to your calendar app, where you pick which calendar it lands in.

⋮ → Create calendar event does the same from an ordinary message — the subject becomes the title and the text becomes the notes — for the mail that says "let's meet Thursday" without a real invitation.

4. Writing mail

✏️ on the phone, New email in the ribbon, or Ctrl+N.

  • To, Cc, Bcc suggest people as you type: your contacts first, then everyone you have written to or heard from. Tap a suggestion to fill it in. The first time, Android asks for permission to read contacts; refuse and the suggestions still come from your mail. Settings → View → Writing can ask again.
  • From is a menu when you have more than one account. Changing it does not swap the signature already in the body.
  • Cc is always there; Bcc appears when you tap it.
  • The body is a proper editor — bold, italic, underline, lists — and on a reply the quoted original can be edited too.
  • 📎 attaches a file, 📋 pastes one from the clipboard, and you can drop a file onto the message from another app in split screen.
  • ➤ sends. Ctrl+Enter does too.

Drafts

Backing out of a half-written message offers Save draft, Discard or Keep writing. Saved drafts go to the server, so they are in Outlook and on the web as well. Tap one in Drafts to carry on. Attachments do not survive that round trip, and the screen says so rather than losing one quietly.

Signatures

Settings → Signatures → Edit opens the same editor the message uses. Paste one from Outlook and it keeps its shape; a logo hosted on a web server is fetched once when you save and kept inside the signature, so it still appears if that server later disappears. The switch decides whether it goes on replies and forwards as well as new messages.

If your organisation adds a signature on its own mail server — CodeTwo and the like — it is added after sending and does not show here. Leave this empty in that case.

In its own window (tablet)

Open in new window on the compose screen moves what you are writing into a window of its own. Settings → View → Writing → Write in a new window makes every new message start that way.

5. Sorting and finding

Sort

⋮ — on the phone in the top bar, on the tablet beside the folder name:

  • Date (newest first) — the default
  • Date (oldest first)
  • Sender
  • Subject

Sender and Subject run A to Z. Subject ignores "Re:" and "Fwd:", so a reply files with the message it answers. The same menu holds Conversations and the row density (Compact, Cozy, Comfortable), and a way through to all view settings.

Search

Tap the search box and type. It searches subject, sender and body on the server, not only what is on the device, so a folder of thousands is fully covered.

  • The scope chooser appears in a real folder: this folder, this account, or everywhere.
  • Results say which folder each hit is in.
  • Long-press a hit to start ticking, then Select all results in the bar, and delete, move, flag or forward as attachment the lot.
  • Results follow whatever sort you have chosen.
  • The ⋮ menu is still there while searching.

6. Tidying up

Swipe

Swipe a row left or right. What each direction does is yours to set (Settings → View → Swipe actions): nothing, delete, move, read/unread, flag or archive. The colour and the word appear as you drag, and on a conversation the word carries the count — "Delete 5" — so you know how much is going before you let go.

Selecting several

Long-press any row to tick it, and keep long-pressing or tapping to add more. The bar at the top replaces the search box and offers: select what is on screen (or all results, in a search), read/unread, flag, forward as attachment, move, delete. Esc or ✕ clears the selection.

With a mouse, right-click gives the full menu for one row without ticking anything.

Copying a message

Right-click → Copy, or Ctrl+C, puts the message on the clipboard as an .eml file. Paste it into a message you are writing to attach it, or into a file manager to keep it.

Quick Steps

Settings → Quick Steps builds named chains — say Mark read, then move to Receipts — and they then appear in the ribbon, in a message's right-click menu, and can be applied in one tap.

7. Folders

  • Long-press a folder for its menu: new subfolder, rename, move, add to Favourites, hide, mark all as read, empty (Trash and Junk only), delete. Each entry only appears where the account allows it.
  • Drag a folder onto another to nest it, onto a folder's edge to reorder it, or onto the account heading to bring it to the top level. Order is kept on this device — IMAP has no such thing.
  • Favourites sit at the top. Hidden folders disappear until you reveal them from the row at the bottom of the tree.
  • The search box at the top of the tree filters the folders themselves.
  • All Inboxes merges every account's Inbox.

8. Being told about new mail

Two separate things, deliberately.

Settings → Sync — how often MyEmail looks:

SettingWhat it costs
Only when I open MyEmailNothing. Nothing reaches you until you open the app.
OccasionallyEasiest on the battery; Android decides when, and overnight that can be much later than asked.
Every 5 minutesA permanent notification, noticeably more battery.
Push, as it arrivesSeconds. A permanent notification, and the most battery.

Settings → Notifications — whether to announce it: a master switch and a mute per account. A muted account still syncs, so its mail is waiting when you open the app.

Tapping a notification opens that message.

9. Home screen widgets

Drop the MyEmail widget on the home screen. It asks which account, then which folder, then whether to count everything or only unread, and lets you name it and pick a colour.

The widget is the size and shape of an app icon: the number top right is what has arrived since you last looked, the number bottom left is what the folder holds. Tapping it opens that folder.

Settings → Home screen widgets changes any of that afterwards.

10. Windows and split screen

Mostly a tablet thing, and it works in DeX.

  • ⋮ → Open in new window on a message, or the ⧉ button while writing.
  • From a full-screen app the new window opens full screen; put the two side by side from Recents ("Open in split screen view"), or drag one in. Already in split screen, the new window takes the other half. In DeX each is simply its own window.
  • Each window is its own copy of MyEmail. They share the mail on the device but not what is selected, which means two messages can be read side by side.
  • In split screen you can drag a message row from one window into the other: it arrives as an .eml file, and dropped onto a folder it is moved there.

12. Settings, screen by screen

Reached from the bottom of the folder tree, or Settings in the ribbon. Each row says what it is currently set to.

  • View — where the reading pane sits (tablet only; on a phone a message always opens as its own screen), writing in a new window, recipient suggestions from contacts, pictures in messages and the senders you trust, conversations, row density, swipe actions, and sort.
  • Sync — how often to look for mail, and what each choice costs.
  • Notifications — whether to announce it, and which accounts to mute.
  • Home screen widgets — what each placed widget counts and what it is called.
  • Quick Steps — build and reorder the action chains.
  • Keyboard shortcuts — the list above.
  • Accounts — add, edit and remove.
  • Signatures — one per account, with the editor.
  • Backup — save these settings to a file, or restore them.
  • About — the version, and checking for a new one.

13. Backup and updates

Backup

Settings → Backup writes a file holding your accounts (without passwords), the folder tree's shape, favourites, hidden folders, Quick Steps, signatures, swipe actions, sort and the senders you trust. Restoring it on a fresh device puts all of that back; you re-enter the passwords.

Updates

Settings → About → Check for updates looks at the releases MyEmail is published to, downloads the new version and installs it. Android asks once for permission to install from MyEmail. The app also notices a new version on its own and says so on the About row.

14. When something looks wrong

A message looks broken or blank. Some senders build mail that needs pictures to make sense. Tap Show images. If it still looks wrong, ⋮ → Save source writes the message exactly as it arrived, which is the thing to look at.

Archive does nothing. Gmail has no Archive folder — "All Mail" is a view, not somewhere to move things to. The swipe says so rather than pretending. Use a folder of your own, or a Quick Step.

An attachment vanished from a draft. Saving a draft to the server does not carry attachments. The screen says so when it happens; re-attach after reopening.

Mail arrives late. Check Settings → Sync. "Occasionally" lets Android decide, and overnight it decides slowly. "Every 5 minutes" or "Push" are the ones that are prompt, and both show a permanent notification because Android will not let a background app do that quietly.

A notification arrived but the message is not in the list. Pull down to sync, or press F9. Mail that a server-side rule filed outside the Inbox is not what the background pass watches.

Two copies in Sent on Microsoft. There should not be: Graph files the sent message itself and MyEmail adds nothing. If you see two, say so.

The widget shows old numbers. It is redrawn by the background sync, so it is as current as Settings → Sync allows. Opening the app refreshes it.

Not seen on a real device yet. Full screen reading, and dragging a message between two windows in split screen, are both built and covered by tests but have not been tried by hand on the tablet. If either misbehaves, that is where to look first.
